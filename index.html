Methodology & Limitations

  • Income tax formula: uses the exact 2026 §32a EStG tariff zones and Vorsorgepauschale mechanism as published in the BMF's official Programmablaufplan (the algorithm behind the official calculator linked above)
  • Social insurance is separate: the official BMF calculator deliberately excludes pension, health, care, and unemployment insurance — this tool adds those using standard 2026 statutory employee rates and contribution ceilings, so the "net salary" figure is complete
  • Statutory health insurance (GKV) assumed — privately insured employees (PKV) are not modeled
  • Church tax base is approximated using the child-adjusted assessment income; minor real-world rounding differences are possible
  • Tax classes V and VI use the official marginal-rate procedure (PAP: MST5_6), not the direct §32a tariff — Class V's Vorsorgepauschale is approximated using the standard formula rather than the official "limited" variant, so results may be slightly generous
  • Class IV with Factor (Faktorverfahren): the official BMF PAP only documents how an already-known factor is applied (multiply the annual tax by it, floor to whole cents) — it does not derive the factor itself, since that's a separate one-time step normally done via your Finanzamt/ELStAM. This tool derives it using the well-established §39f EStG formula (F = joint splitting tax ÷ sum of both spouses' standard Class IV tax), reusing the same joint-splitting and Class IV logic validated elsewhere in this suite — not a figure taken directly from the official calculator's own source
  • Always cross-check important decisions against the official BMF Steuerrechner or a licensed tax advisor (Steuerberater)

Methodology & Assumptions

  • Net worth, not monthly payment: the real comparison is home equity (value minus remaining mortgage, minus selling costs if selling) vs. an invested portfolio — a lower monthly payment doesn't automatically mean buying wins
  • Symmetric opportunity cost: whichever side has lower costs in a given year is assumed to invest the difference at the return rate above — so the renter's down payment (and any ongoing savings) grow in the market, and the same applies to the buyer if their costs are ever lower
  • One-time purchase costs are never recovered — transfer tax, notary, and agent fees don't add to home equity, which is why renting starts ahead by that amount
  • Fixed-rate mortgage assumed for the full term; no refinancing modeled
  • Mortgage interest is not tax-deductible in this model — generally not deductible for an owner-occupied home in Germany (unlike some other countries)
  • Always get a personalized comparison from a financial advisor before a home purchase decision this significant
